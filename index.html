THE NEW NORMAL

The ceiling kept leaking teeth again.

Not water —
teeth.

They fell one by one into the sink
with the sound of dice inside a skull,
clogging the drain.

Mother said the pipes were old.

I asked her why the walls
were breathing so loudly at night.

She did not answer.
She kept stitching raw meat into my shirt sleeves
with fishing wire
while humming lullabies backwards.

Every dawn
the crows gathered outside my window
and beat themselves against the glass
until feathers and blood pasted into a halo.

The neighbors complained about the smell.

I stopped noticing it after the third week.

There are fingers in the flowerpots now.
Tiny ones.
Blue at the nails.
Growing like roots.

I think they belong to the child
that keeps standing at the edge of my hallway
with his jaw hanging open like a broken cupboard.

He never moves.

Sometimes I wake up with mud in my lungs
and cemetery grass beneath my tongue,
my ribs tied together with wet hair,
my stomach full of black river water.

But everyone says I look healthy.

Yesterday, a dog followed me home.

It whimpered.

Not at me —

at the trail behind me.

A long dark stain
threading down the street
thick as coagulated wine.

I checked my shoes.

No blood.

Only dirt.

Only worms.

And the occasional fingernail.

At dinner, Father carved into the roast
and maggots spilled out steaming, writhing, blind.
Nobody reacted.

Mother kept eating.

My sister asked me why I never blink anymore.

I laughed so hard
something cracked inside my throat.

A beetle crawled out.

They still did not notice.

At night
I hear someone digging.

Closer each evening.

Closer.

Closer.

The sound comes from beneath my bed now.

No —

beneath me.

And every morning
I wake with more soil packed inside my ears.

Tonight I finally looked in the bathroom mirror.

The mirror looked back late.

Very late.

Like it needed time
to remember my shape.

And when it did —

I saw the skin slipping from my face
in soft grey ribbons,
my left eye clouded with fungus,
my neck opened to the spine
like a second mouth.

I stood there for a long time.

Trying to understand
why the maggots inside my cheek
spelled my name when they moved.

Then I remembered —

the road,

the rain,

the headlights folding inward,

and somebody screaming from beneath the tires.

The strange part is —

I still don't know
if it was me
or the thing that crawled out afterward.

IF I WERE STONE

If I Were Stone

would you love me
if I were stone

laid beneath the patient earth
my name cut into silence
my breath traded
for a permanence I could never earn

would your hands grow gentle then
upon my cold and speechless skin
as though I had at last
become something worthy of care

for now
I am too much of movement
too much of fracture
a restless thing
forever unshaped

I falter in your presence
I diminish in your absence
I am neither absence nor form
but an error
persisting

yet stone—
stone does not fail

it does not speak unwisely
nor love imperfectly

it endures
and in enduring
it is forgiven.

tell me—
would my silence then be called peace

would my distance be named dignity
my stillness be named strength

would you stand above me
and soften your voice
as though I had always been gentle

it is a strange mercy
this kindness granted to the unmoving

how the ruined are remembered
not as they were
but as they can no longer be

and I—
I grow weary of becoming

tell me—
would you praise me then?

call me calm
call me gentle
call me anything
that requires my silence

because now
every word I speak
arrives misshapen
like bone pushing through skin

I have seen the way
your gaze withdraws
not sharply
never cruelly
but as one steps back
from something that might collapse

and I do
continuously

a structure unsound
aware of its own fracture

I have tried to remain
yet remaining has grown cruel

I have always been
half-decayed in spirit

there is a longing in me
not for death
but for the absence of failing

for a state
where I can no longer betray
what little is left of me

stone does not betray

it simply is

and perhaps that is enough
to be loved

or at least
no longer refused

so tell me—

if I were laid to rest
and the earth took from me
all that trembles and errs

if I were reduced
to weight
to silence
to something that cannot wound

would you love me then

or would it only seem so
because I would no longer be here
to prove you wrong.

911

Prologue: 911 it's an emergency.

The morning begins with something ordinary.

A cup on the table.
Breads, butter, a knife.
Steam rising in a thin white thread.
The window slightly open,
curtains breathing in and out

A bird lands on the railing.

Nothing unusual.

The spoon circles the cup
once
twice
three times—

and the sound does not stop.

It keeps circling
even after the spoon is still.

Clink.
Clink.
Clink.

The walls lean closer to listen.

The bird on the railing
tilts its head
and its eye becomes
a black hole
where the room begins to fall.

wtf!?

The tea tastes metallic.

Like licking a coin
Something inside the cup
moves.

I look at my hands.

Why is there blood on my hands?
How? When? Why?

The room begins to breathe wrong.

The clock melts into soft ticking flesh.
Each second drips onto the floor
and crawls away.

The bird outside the window
has multiplied.

Five of them now.

Ten.

Twenty.

All staring.

All with my face.

THE FUCK!??

A whisper grows inside my skull.

Open it.

I don’t know what it is.

But the whisper keeps scratching
like fingernails
inside the brain.

OPEN IT.

The spoon bends.

The cup cracks.

The table begins bleeding..

My reflection in the window
is slightly delayed.

It smiles
three seconds late.

Then it doesn't stop smiling.

The whisper says
there are wires inside the skin.

Pull one.

Just one.

See what happens.

So I listen.

Fingernails become tools.

The body becomes a locked drawer
and curiosity
is a crowbar.

Something opens.

A long red hallway
stretching somewhere
deeper than the ribs.

Footsteps echo there.

Not mine.

CRASH!!

The bird crashes through the glass.

Except the window never broke.

Except the bird is inside my mouth.

Except I am watching this
from the ceiling.

The room has teeth now.

The floor slowly chewing
on my shadow.

The walls growing taller
like prison guards.

And the whisper has become a choir.

Break it.

Break everything.

I stab the silence.

The knife?
Where did the knife come from?

My chest bursts like rotten fruit.

Inside are smaller hearts
still beating
still whispering.

Someone is standing behind me.

I know because my spine
suddenly grows cold fingers.

I turn.

It is me.

But hollow.

Eyes like burnt-out tunnels.

Holding a knife made of a mirror.

I can see myself in the mirror.

We stare.

One of us laughs.

One of us begs.

One of us dies.

I can't tell
which one I am.

Epilogue:

The cup is still on the table.

Steam rising
calm
quiet
ordinary.

The spoon rests beside it.

Outside, the bird returns to the railing.

Nothing unusual.

Except
A chair waits at the table.
Someone is sitting in it.
Almost.

The eyes are gone.
Two small hollows
staring at nothing.

Head tilted slightly
like a question,

The hands hang loosely
over the arms of the chair.
They are quiet now.

The knife is still there;
Just....
Buried in the chest.

THE DISCIPLINE OF LOSING

Prologue:

There was a time when I did not fear gentleness.
I remember it faintly—

Carrying no caution in my chest,
What I gave, I gave as though
the world were incapable of breaking it.

—

What followed
did not arrive as ruin;
but with a patience
that outlived who I was.

It did not leave me shattered.
It left me aware.

And that, perhaps,
was worse.

—

Now, whenever warmth comes close,
I find myself returning
to what once bled—

not to heal it,
but to feel it again.

I turn those scars
back into gaping wounds,
as though pain were proof
of me - still being me.

I do not know when this became instinct,
or why I no longer resist it.

Perhaps I have learned
to mistrust anything
that does not hurt enough.

And so it always ends the same.

Not in violence,
not in betrayal—

but in a quiet withdrawal,
as though something sacred
realized it had been placed
in the wrong hands.

—

I do not blame what leaves.

The fault:
It lives within me—
in the way I reach,
in the way I retreat,
in the way I undo
what I cannot bear to keep.

—

To love is to lose—

to lose myself
in the memory
of who I used to be,

to lose her
to the distance
I create with my own hands,

to lose trust
in anything that dares
to feel certain,

to lose everything
long before it is taken
from me.

—

And somewhere beneath it all,
I feel the quiet grief
of the one I used to be—

the boy —
who did not know
how to ruin this.
who did not stand
at the edge of something beautiful
already preparing
for its end.

—

I do not hate
who I have become.
That would be easier.

—

I only grieve him.
In the silence
that follows every almost.

—

Epilogue:

I have simply learned
how to lose
with such consistency
that it no longer feels
like loss at all.

—

Only inevitability.

THE INNER MONOLOGUE

Prologue:

Before anything is noticed, it has already begun.

There is no clear starting point.
Just awareness
arriving a second too late.

The room exists.
That is certain.
A table.
A chair.
A body sitting in it.
All present.
All correct.
Yet something feels…
IDK.

The ceiling fan spins
slow
uneven—

like it is tired
of holding the room together.

Nothing announces collapse.

No alarms.
No sirens.

Just a small crack
somewhere behind the ribs.

—

I sit there.

Breathing correctly.(maybe)
Looking normal.(maybe)
Performing existence
with professional precision.

—

A thought slips in.

Soft.

Polite.

You were built for this.

—

The walls don’t close in.
They simply step back.

Giving me space
to fall properly.

—

I remember things
I never chose:

The way my voice hesitates
like it expects to be punished.

The way my hands
forget what they were reaching for.

The way mirrors
never argue back.

—

There is no villain here.

No shadow in the doorway.
No whisper behind the ear.

Just me.

Standing
exactly where the damage begins.

—

The realization isn’t loud.

It settles.

Like dust in the lungs.

Like truth
that has been waiting patiently.

—

If I were to meet utter destruction,
no one will even take the blame.

It was me after all.

It has always been me.

—

Because the evidence
would be embarrassingly clear.

Fingerprints
everywhere.

Mine.

—

I would stand in the wreckage
like an architect
pretending surprise.

Pathetic.
Not as an insult.
As a specification.

Shameful.
Not as judgment.
As a feature.

Abhorrent.
Not as accident.
As intention.

—

I try to resist the thought.

But resistance feels rehearsed.

Like even rebellion
was pre-installed.

—

A laugh escapes.

Dry.

Hollow.

Too accurate.

—

Of course.

Of course it was me.

Who else could sustain this
so consistently?

—

The room doesn’t collapse.

It simply agrees.

—

And in that agreement,

something settles into place.

Perfectly.

Terrifyingly.

Correct.

—

Epilogue:

No one came to investigate.

The chair remains where I left it.
Me-
Untouched.

The glass still half-filled.
The fan still turning
like nothing learned anything.

—

Only a quiet conclusion
resting in the air:

This was not a tragedy.

This was compliance.

—

And I—

I am still here.

Unbroken.

Because this
was never damage.

—

This
was design.

THE ALTAR

I arrive at the altar empty-handed,
pockets turned inside out like skinned birds.
My ribs leaning forward, begging.

I want warmth
with frostbitten palms.
I want shelter
while termites chew my spine into sawdust.

Love is a language,
It only speaks in wounds.

They said love teaches endurance:
how much red the body can afford
before the lights dim,
before the heart starts whispering its resignation.

And yet like a fool,
Every night I fall for it.

Craving a mouth
while my own is sewn shut with guilt.
Craving touch
with hands that tremble like crime scenes.

Irony is a quiet god:
it watches me starve
while hoarding hunger itself.
It laughs softly when I ask for mercy
with teeth already gnawing on my tongue.

Love is a cruel god:
It drags its knuckles through my head,
hangs my thoughts from hooks
to see which ones scream the longest.
Sometimes it is gentle,
a lullaby hummed by a butcher
washing his hands too slowly.
Sometimes it is sudden,
a mirror shattering mid-sentence,
finally telling the truth.

Now I know:
wanting is not innocence.
Need is not purity.
And bleeding is not proof of devotion;
just evidence that the lesson worked.

Still, here I am,
asking for light
with a body built entirely of shadows,
wondering why the door keeps opening
into my own chest.

THE PARADOX

the paradox feeds on itself—
split creature, double-jawed—
one mouth swallowing light,
the other vomiting it back as ash.

I walk inside its pulse:
rooms flickering between overfilled
and
scraped clean,
sometimes both,
same breath.

hands reach for me—
I recoil into wanting,
lurch forward into erasing—
a motion that breaks my silhouette
into two contradicting shadows
fighting over which one is real.

mirrors grind their teeth:
they show me hollow,
they show me overflowing,
the glass bleeding
because it cannot decide.

fruit bursts open on the floor—
half of it rots instantly,
half grows teeth—
each half gnawing the other
in worship of the contradiction.

kindness arrives like a gentle saw:
one stroke carving me open,
the next stitching me shut,
thread looping through bone,
tightening around the impossible shape
of wanting what I cannot hold
and holding what I cannot want.

in the center of the labyrinth—
the paradox sits on a throne of its own ribs,
beckoning me with a hand
that points in two directions at once.

I kneel.
the hand splits my chest
to plant something there—
a seed that screams
and purrs
and screams
as it grows.

and the whisper through the wound
tastes like metal and milk:

you are too full to feel it
you are too empty not to.
you are too incomplete to lack it
you are too overflowing to find it
you are the famine
you are the feast
you are the absence shaped like abundance
you are the abundance shaped like absence.

NOSTALGIA

It’s funny, isn’t it—
how at twenty, I already ache
like a man who’s lived six decades
too long.

I scroll through my gallery
and it feels like flipping through obituaries—
not of people,
but of versions of me
who smiled wider, slept deeper,
and believed the world was kinder.

There was a time I laughed
without worrying if it echoed wrong.
When dark circles weren’t medals of effort
but just the shadows that came after laughter.
When insomnia was just a fancy word
I found in someone’s sad poem—
and not my 3 A.M. voices.

I miss that boy.
The one who thought the world
was a story he could rewrite with crayons.
Who didn’t know that sometimes
the ink dries out
long before the story ends.

I miss grandpa—
his voice a campfire of warmth,
crackling tales of his prime—
his eyes brighter than the moonlight
that used to sneak through the window.
Now it’s just me and the hum of the fan,
and sometimes,
even that sounds like him breathing.

Grandma still makes that sweet—
you know, the one sticky enough
to hold together three generations.
I ate it last week,
and it tasted like the childhood days—
soft, slow,
and heartbreakingly short.

I even miss my old enemy—
the school nemesis,
the one who once flushed my specs
down the toilet
like some grand rebellion.
Now I laugh,
but back then it was war,
and every bruise had purpose.
Funny how some enemies
teach you more about loyalty.
We are the best bros now.

Cycle rides—
wind screaming, chain creaking,
heart racing like it believed in forever.
We never raced to win,
we raced because time hadn’t learned
to chase us yet.

And that brother—
always there, shoulder to shoulder,
even when silence
was the loudest thing between us.

And those KFC days—
saving every coin
just to taste a luxury dipped in friendship.
The crispy skin of youth—
now stale without him.
They changed the recipe, I think.
Or maybe we did.

The iPod days—
earphones plugged in.
2019—Bieber, Mendes, and teenage heartbreak.
And those Roald Dahl, Geronimo Stilton nights—
when stories fixed everything,
and even villains felt friendly.

Old friends—
we were pure chaos in school shoes,
laughing so loud even the walls joined in.
Remember that slang poem we wrote?
half roast, half nonsense—
that got us a VIP invite
to the principal’s office?
We walked in like rappers,
came out like clowns with slips
and zero regrets.
Now they post quotes about “healing” and “cutting ties,”
and I just laugh—
When did rebels turn into this?

And that girl—
God, that girl—
who said I looked good
with my sleeves folded.
Funny how I still do it,
like a superstition I never unlearned.
Sometimes I wonder
if she’d still say it now—
when my wrists carry time
more than touch.

Even yesterday feels like
a sepia photograph I’ve already lost.
I remember something I did—
and it already feels like history.

Now I am just another cog—
working, studying, working, eating,
sleeping like it’s a myth
someone forgot to prove.
Life has become
Ctrl + C, Ctrl + V.

And yet—
somewhere between deadlines and caffeine,
I still pause at a sunset,
still smile at old songs,
still crave the taste of simplicity.
Is it a sin
to be homesick for moments
that still live inside me?

Because nostalgia—
she visits like rain on a summer night,
uninvited,
